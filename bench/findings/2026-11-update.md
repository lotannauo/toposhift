# Storage findings: November 2026 update

11 November 2026; synthesizes the registered readings completed through 24 October. This update adds later evidence to the [historical report](REPORT.md), without rerunning its experiments or replacing its conclusions at their original scope. The tested commits and reproduction limits are [below](#evidence-and-reproduction).

The evidence supports a specific benefit from per-entity history with checkpoints. It also narrows the larger bet: native interval tables can bound read work, and a useful storage layout does not by itself establish a good complete investigation system. Background retention remains an operating question, with an adverse observation already recorded.

These are research configurations. They are not performance claims about v0.1.0 or the current `serve`/MCP path. A later benchmark implementation need not be in a released binary for its result to be useful; the result still belongs to its measured commit and configuration.

## What changed our mind

L is the per-entity log layout with derived checkpoints; M is the per-edge MVCC layout using CockroachDB-style keys. Both were implemented in the benchmark engine. Native baselines instead use each database's own representation. They are different comparisons.

| Question | What the completed evidence supports | Consequence and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Do checkpoints help historical reads? | In the CI warm-read study, checkpointed L had lower timings than M and L without checkpoints in every registered primary hot reverse-read cell. Some secondary cells favored M. | Keep the narrow layout/checkpoint benefit. This was a full seven-day synthetic spike store, warm cache and one reader; it was not `serve`, MCP or a native-database ranking. [Scope](#warm-read-comparison) |
| Does that establish an advantage over native interval indexes? | Bulk-loaded Postgres GiST interval tables met the registered bounded-work rule on the judged projected synthetic populations, in both loads. | The earlier read-work advantage over per-edge versioning does not extend to these interval tables. This is work-growth evidence, not elapsed-time superiority. [Scope](#native-postgres) |
| Is background retention ready for serving? | The report-only product-store canary observed batch-latency tails above its printed reference lines, with checkpoints on and off. | Investigate the intended serving configuration. The adapter run had no boot key and grants no store-gate verdict; it does not isolate checkpoints as the cause. [Scope](#background-retention) |
| Can a metrics store answer simpler placement questions? | VictoriaMetrics matched the compared sample-resolution answers under the registered mapping. | Keep the narrower contract visible: earlier-knowledge questions were unsupported; boundary/deleted-fact reads were excluded, and two synthetic reverse families were not compared under the registered rule. [Scope](#victoriametrics) |
| Does a small columnar archive justify a cold tier? | DuckDB's final version passed its local answer/repeatability checks, but interval-file work growth was inconclusive and did not meet the all-bounded condition. | No cold tier is indicated by this registered layout and rule. The result was not a correctness failure or a finding of linear growth; CI timing/sealing and part of the byte comparison remain unevaluated. [Scope](#duckdb) |
| Can native history represent both time axes? | The implemented XTDB and Dolt mappings answered the registered small-input questions. | Native alternatives are credible. Preserve each mapping's client work, retention limits and larger-input stops; these are capability findings, not a speed ranking. [Scope](#xtdb-and-dolt) |
| Should compression tuning be adopted? | The Zstd and Balanced trials did not meet their registered adoption conditions. | These trials did not justify adoption. A completed trial is not a successful candidate, and these diagnostic figures are not for public quotation. [Scope](#compression) |

The important change is from a broad superiority claim to a decision with conditions. Checkpointed history is a serious implementation for selected historical reads. Postgres supplies a competent native alternative; some other systems answer a subset or need additional client interpretation. None of these studies measures the full cost of the same operator investigation across complete systems.

The studies also changed priorities. Repeating warm L/M reads or widening a compression sweep would add less information than resolving the serving-mode retention issue and testing one complete investigation. A native bitemporal Postgres design remains an open comparator: the measured interval table used a client log fold for older knowledge tokens.

## What we still need to learn

- **Usefulness:** can someone answer a real infrastructure question more accurately or with less effort than through their existing workflow? A staged demo or an answer-equality check does not establish that.
- **Complete task cost:** compare one declared investigation, such as window co-residency, against a competent native implementation. Agree on observations, interval semantics, knowledge state and independent expected answers before measuring cost.
- **Serving and retention:** identify the bounded change to test under the intended boot-key, write and query configuration. The adverse adapter result is evidence to investigate, not a result to replace with a favorable microbenchmark.

The proposed next examples are an impossible-in-time path, changed placement with distinct replacement identities, and delayed evidence that changes the latest answer while preserving an earlier token's answer. A reproducible investigation should carry its question, window, token, applicable policy and expected interpretation. That would connect the research to an inspectable answer without implying that arbitrary queries have an independent verifier.

## Evidence and reproduction

This is a synthesis of the registered readings and their superseding errata. No experiment was rerun for this update; the linked figure reformats recorded CI results. Timing/rate/memory publication remains restricted to permitted CI results; local evidence is restricted to permitted repeatable deterministic counts and answers. Work counters from different engines are not interchangeable and are not combined into a leaderboard.

The source code and CI runs below are public. The registration log, some local orchestration/checking scripts and retained result files are outside the repository. Consequently, these links let readers inspect the implementation and available CI record, but are not a complete public reproduction package. No claim of independent replication follows from reviewing those records. Local raw outputs remain uncommitted.

### Warm-read comparison

[CI run 39769897468](https://github.com/lotannauo/toposhift/actions/runs/39769897468), commit [`e85ba569626ede3e920814b7ba915ba525d68b1b`](https://github.com/lotannauo/toposhift/commit/e85ba569626ede3e920814b7ba915ba525d68b1b). Spike engine; full seven-day synthetic `rmae` store, preset `ci`, seed `1`; L/k64a2l5m, M/crdb1 and L/off. Warm 256 MiB block cache, no misses during timed asks, one reader, three warmups and 31 timed asks per query, five jobs per candidate and architecture. Times include engine counter-reporting calls with the recorder muted, exclude answer hashing, and retain normal garbage collection. Stores were noncanonical, Sync off, with no Go memory limit. This was not `pebblestore` under `serve`'s boot key or the MCP path. The x86 jobs pooled AMD EPYC 7763, AMD EPYC 9V45 and Intel Xeon Platinum 8573C unevenly across candidates; aarch64 Neoverse-N2 is the cleaner comparison.

The primary cells were hot service/node reverse neighbors at now, 3h, 9h and 1d. The [figure and numerical table](2026-11-warm-reads.md) show all primary aarch64 cells, their job variation, the control and secondary qualifications. Secondary outcomes are not universal L wins. The [recorded protocol and offline-judge command](https://github.com/lotannauo/toposhift/blob/e85ba569626ede3e920814b7ba915ba525d68b1b/bench/README.md#timed-reads-on-ci-hardware-read--timed-and-readtiming) describe how to read compatible CI artifacts. Input plan SHA-256: `47a9455198362119829b30e4c75f003c513ace26aac60b82a12646a10c2723c1`; stream digest: `93d02559ff842f1300afbc41c9bf8ea45cce6439c176b0d34dee156c659f9317`. No new dispatch is required to read the recorded result.

### Native Postgres

Postgres 17.11 at [`e85ba569626ede3e920814b7ba915ba525d68b1b`](https://github.com/lotannauo/toposhift/commit/e85ba569626ede3e920814b7ba915ba525d68b1b), using `int8range` intervals and GiST indexes after bulk loading and finishing vacuum. The projected synthetic plans retained 2, 7, 14 and 30 days. Work means index entries returned plus rows removed by filter/recheck, classified by the registered growth rule. GiST counters depend on build order; it is the classification across both loads that held, not identical raw counters.

Latest-token point reads used interval tables; older-token point reads and windows used the log. The skilled streaming writer had answer and writer/retention checks, but no measured cold read-work result after ongoing writes. The [driver and command entry points](https://github.com/lotannauo/toposhift/blob/e85ba569626ede3e920814b7ba915ba525d68b1b/bench/postgres/cmd/pgspike/main.go) are public. The registered local driver `pgc-run.sh` and readers `pgc-check.py`/`pgc-class.py` are not published here.

### Background retention

[CI run 39624099848](https://github.com/lotannauo/toposhift/actions/runs/39624099848), commit [`c6083dd2176d170feac5d26fdf057846c161ad84`](https://github.com/lotannauo/toposhift/commit/c6083dd2176d170feac5d26fdf057846c161ad84). `pebblestore` through the benchmark adapter, no boot key, background retention, Sync on, paced at 4,500 records/s, flush threshold 32,768, checkpoints k64a2l5m and checkpoints-off control; synthetic `rmae`, preset `ci`, seed `1`, seven-day window and three repetitions per architecture. Version-three store-gate rules were printed for reference; this was explicitly not a gate verdict. One arm64 longest-batch row was partial because its outside-window runner control could not be judged. Neither a 30-day result nor a `serve`/MCP latency claim follows. See the [store-gate command and rules](https://github.com/lotannauo/toposhift/blob/c6083dd2176d170feac5d26fdf057846c161ad84/bench/README.md#the-stores-retention-gate-storegate).

### VictoriaMetrics

VictoriaMetrics v1.153.0 at [`e85ba569626ede3e920814b7ba915ba525d68b1b`](https://github.com/lotannauo/toposhift/commit/e85ba569626ede3e920814b7ba915ba525d68b1b). The native representation used labeled samples, deletion markers, watch-state re-exports and producer-specific lookback windows. Inputs were the projected two/seven-day synthetic plans and one Alibaba placement hour at 300-second sampling, in its every-sample form. Synthetic node-reverse and service-reverse families were reported not compared at sample resolution because too many questions were excluded. This does not establish the exact temporal or multi-hop contract for a metrics database. The [driver](https://github.com/lotannauo/toposhift/blob/e85ba569626ede3e920814b7ba915ba525d68b1b/bench/victoria/cmd/vmspike/main.go) is public; the registered local `vc-run.sh` and `vc-check.sh` are not published here.

### DuckDB

DuckDB v1.5.6 reading Parquet at [`0bc496c941c0273e1056b7e718f2e5317fa5b045`](https://github.com/lotannauo/toposhift/commit/0bc496c941c0273e1056b7e718f2e5317fa5b045); the Alibaba interval-counter rereads used [`2a1b1a7019fb19f34ff4f912d1ed38fcbf2a6b87`](https://github.com/lotannauo/toposhift/commit/2a1b1a7019fb19f34ff4f912d1ed38fcbf2a6b87). One thread; configured memory limit 4GB. Synthetic inputs were the four projected plans; Alibaba inputs were placement at 300/60-second cadences. The final reading follows version two and its environment erratum, which superseded narrow footer-metadata equality and scan-accounting refusals; the earlier invalid statuses were not wrong-answer results. The interval arm still needs record files sorted both ways for windows and older tokens. The [driver](https://github.com/lotannauo/toposhift/blob/0bc496c941c0273e1056b7e718f2e5317fa5b045/bench/duckdb/cmd/duckspike/main.go) is public; the registered `dc-run.sh` and `dc-check-v2.sh` are not published here.

### XTDB and Dolt

XTDB 2.1.0 at [`e85ba569626ede3e920814b7ba915ba525d68b1b`](https://github.com/lotannauo/toposhift/commit/e85ba569626ede3e920814b7ba915ba525d68b1b) supplied native valid-time indexing and system-time reads over lifecycle segments computed by the baseline planner. The two-day projected plan's answers matched, including earlier-knowledge questions. It retained the whole stream rather than implementing the plan's horizons, and built no multi-hop queries. Its seven-day attempt reached the local cap and was void; larger-input capability was not measured. [Driver](https://github.com/lotannauo/toposhift/blob/e85ba569626ede3e920814b7ba915ba525d68b1b/bench/xtdb/cmd/xtspike/main.go).

Dolt 2.4.2 at [`e922c9a7bf7188b18eb82367eddac70b76e9c19a`](https://github.com/lotannauo/toposhift/commit/e922c9a7bf7188b18eb82367eddac70b76e9c19a) answered that plan using committed state plus a log, at an 8 GiB container limit without garbage collection. Some older-token event-time questions still needed log records; treating commit time as event time gave incorrect answers. Its seven-day load and fresh Alibaba-hour pass did not finish under the configured memory limit. No larger-input count or comparative speed follows from those stops. [Driver](https://github.com/lotannauo/toposhift/blob/e922c9a7bf7188b18eb82367eddac70b76e9c19a/bench/dolt/cmd/doltspike/main.go).

The local `xt-run.sh`/`xt-check.sh` and `dc-run.sh`/`dc-check-v2.sh` orchestration/readers are not published here. These studies provide no repeatable directory-byte result and no measured internal-engine work ranking.
